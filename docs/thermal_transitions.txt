Thermal startup/shutdown correction -- thermal_transitions_v1
===========================================================

Scope
The same build_uc_model is used by the direct MILP and every LP/MILP repair
stage. Existing scenario inputs, trained weights and campaign results are not
modified. DR remains load_shifting_v1. The binary learning interface retains
its seven channels; shutdown is derived, not an additional prediction target.

Convention
R is the existing online ramp in MW per model period (no new time scaling).
SU and SD are separate MW limits at startup and immediately before shutdown.
Default: SU = SD = min(Pmax, max(Pmin, R)). Thus a 100 MW unit with Pmin=45
and R=20 can start at 45 MW, then reach 65 MW, ramp back to 45 MW, then stop.
It cannot jump straight from 0 to 100 MW or stop directly from 90 MW.

This is an explicit one-period startup/shutdown approximation. It does not
describe startup trajectories lasting several periods, nor minimum up/down
times. Initial output remains zero in the generated scenarios. For an already
running ScenarioData unit, initial commitment is inferred from positive initial
output, or supplied explicitly via thermal_initial_on. Initial states must
respect Pmin/Pmax. An initially running unit has no spurious startup cost.

Optional scenario tech keys thermal_startup_limit_pct and
thermal_shutdown_limit_pct specify SU/Pmax and SD/Pmax (fractions, not 0--100).
ScenarioData also accepts per-zone thermal_startup_limit and
thermal_shutdown_limit dictionaries in MW. Limits below Pmin or above Pmax
are rejected for this one-period model rather than silently clipped.

Equations
u[t] is commitment; v[t] is startup; w[t] is shutdown.
v[t] >= u[t]-u[t-1]; v[t] <= u[t]; v[t] <= 1-u[t-1].
w[t] = u[t-1]-u[t]+v[t] (derived expression).
Pmin*u[t] <= p[t] <= Pmax*u[t].
p[t]-p[t-1] <= R*u[t-1] + SU*v[t].
p[t-1]-p[t] <= R*u[t] + SD*w[t].
At t=0, the previous state is the declared initial state. Exact startup
constraints prevent false transitions from relaxing ramps even at zero cost.

Repairs and warm starts
All five repair stages inherit these constraints from the shared builder.
Round-and-refix derives startups from rounded commitment instead of rounding
startup independently. Heuristic warm starts share the same thermal limits,
preserve a genuine zero online ramp and continue ramping down if shutdown is
not yet possible. They remain heuristics, not full-network certificates.
The latent repair boundary checks the saved thermal trajectories independently
and refuses to publish a finite candidate objective when they violate them.
